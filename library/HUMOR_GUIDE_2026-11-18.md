# Humor & writing style — Ryan Figures It Out (from the user, 2026-11-18)

The script must have genuinely clever, witty humor: sharp comedy, not forced comedy.

Use a natural mix of:
- **Clever wordplay and puns** that are smart, unexpected and feel effortless (never a groan-pun on the topic's obvious word).
- **Sarcasm and dry humor:** subtle, sharp, slightly cynical remarks (Maya's home turf).
- **Witty observations:** funny takes on everyday situations, human behavior, irony and absurdity.
- **Clever analogies and comparisons:** unexpected comparisons that land because they're surprisingly accurate.
- **Deadpan:** saying ridiculous things with a completely serious tone (Ryan's sincerity, Maya's understatement).
- **Subtle roasts:** playful, intelligent jabs that never feel mean.

Never:
- cringe, cheesy, childish or predictable jokes;
- forced punchlines, or jokes inserted just to be funny;
- overused internet humor, generic memes or recycled one-liners;
- excessive exclamation marks or exaggerated reactions;
- obvious jokes that insult the audience's intelligence;
- corny setups followed by predictable punchlines.

**Golden rule:** make the audience smirk, chuckle or think "okay, that was actually clever."
- Humor grows out of the conversation and the facts. It's never bolted on.
- Cleverness over quantity: one genuinely good line beats five mediocre ones.
- Keep it conversational, natural, sharp and entertaining. Never sacrifice the content for a joke.

**Review pass before finalizing:** read every joke. If it sounds like a generic AI joke, a dad joke, or something trying too hard, rewrite it or cut it. Witty, not goofy. Clever, not cringe.
