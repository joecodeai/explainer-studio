DESCRIPTION
In 1942, a cheerful dentist from Pennsylvania wrote to President Roosevelt with a plan: catch millions of bats, strap tiny firebombs to them, and drop them over Japan. Roosevelt's reply: "This man is not a nut."

What followed was two years, two million dollars, a top-secret dinner party, a bomber full of shrieking bats, a rancher who swore he'd never talk, and one brand-new US airfield that burned to the ground in fifteen minutes.

This is the true story of Project X-Ray, told with the words of the people who were there, plus our own read on it: why the plan didn't sound crazy in January 1942, what the fire really proved, and why we think it was really cancelled. We read the first-hand accounts side by side; where they disagree, we say so.
