# Episode outline: "Benny Became a Millionaire in Japan (He Didn't)" (about 11 min)

**Format (from now on, every YouTube video):** Benny goes somewhere and does something with real stakes, makes believable mistakes, and Maya untangles them through conversation. Every fact arrives through a situation, discovery or interaction. No lectures. By the end the viewer understands the topic without feeling taught.

All numbers below come from the sources in research.json. Story details (the fee, the ramen price, the lucky cat) are fiction set in the real world.

---

### Cold open: the airport exchange counter (0:00–0:45)
- Benny slides $1,000 across the counter. The clerk counts out a thick stack: "After our fee, one hundred and fifty-six thousand yen."
- Benny freezes, then turns to Maya. "Maya. Are you sitting down?" / "I'm standing, Benny. We're in an airport."
- "I'm a millionaire." / "You're a hundred-and-fifty-six-thousand-aire." / "That's *so* close."
- **Hook:** what he has is a real number of yen. What it really means is the episode.

### Act 1: "rich" at the ramen shop (0:45–3:00)
- **Ticket machine:** Benny presses the most expensive button, the ¥1,500 special, three times ("one for each of us, and one for confidence").
- **The tip:** he tries to tip the chef. The chef politely slides the coins back, and Benny tries again. Maya explains you don't tip in Japan: "He's not being modest. He's being polite."
- **The maths:** ¥1,500 is about $9.40, because one dollar buys almost 160 yen, the most in more than two months. Shown on a map.
- **The sale:** "So Japan is on sale." / "For you."
- **Tourists:** a record 42.7 million foreign visitors came in 2025 and spent a record ¥9.5 trillion. This year visitors spent a record ¥7.06 trillion by September.
- **Tag:** "So I'm part of a trend." / "You're part of a statistic."

### Act 2: why is the yen so cheap? A late-night konbini (3:00–5:30)
- **Benny's theory:** "They made the money too pretty, so they printed too much."
- **Maya's demo:** two coffee cups, the US and Japan. Money flows to the cup that pays more. US prices rose 0.4% in October (forecast 0.3%), so markets see a 100% chance the Fed raises rates in December. The dollar index hit a 19-month high.
- **Callback:** "Money goes where it's treated best." / "Like you and the bank with the free pen." / "It was a *really* good pen."

### Act 3: Tokyo strikes back, at the hotel (5:30–7:30)
- **The warning:** Japan's top currency official, Atsushi Mimura, comes on the TV: the moves are "too rapid", and it's "too early to forget what happened this summer."
- **Benny:** "What happened this summer?!"
- **Maya explains intervention:** Japan sold dollars to buy yen.
  - It spent ¥15.4 trillion on July 30, July 31 and August 3; July 30 alone was a record ¥7.98 trillion.
  - That pushed the year's total past ¥27 trillion, beating the previous record of about ¥15 trillion (2024). Part of it was done jointly with the US.
  - Within a month the yen slid back toward 160.
- **Tag:** "Markets don't send thank-you notes."
- **Benny's plan:** "If they do it again, my sale ends. I have to spend everything. Tonight." / Maya: "Nobody knows when. Not even Tokyo."
- **Montage:** the souvenir shop and a giant lucky cat.

### Act 4: the other side, the ramen chef (7:30–9:30)
- **Chef's story:** the chef mentions his costs. A weak yen makes imported fuel, food and raw materials more expensive. Wholesale prices rose 7.1% in October. The government is debating cutting the tax on food from 8% to 1%. The economy shrank last quarter for the first time in four quarters.
- **Benny's realisation (quietly):** "So my sale is his price rise."
- **Benny is right:** "So if Japan raises interest rates, the yen gets stronger. My sale ends… but his flour gets cheaper." / Maya, surprised: "…Benny. That's actually right." / "I know things." The Bank of Japan is widely expected to raise rates in December.

### Ending: back at the exchange counter (9:30–11:00)
- **Changing back:** "I'd like to change my yen back." He has ¥400 and a giant lucky cat. "That's about two dollars, sir."
- **Callback:** "I was a millionaire for five days." / "A hundred-and-fifty-six-thousand-aire." / "Still so close."
- **Comment prompt:** "What would *you* buy first with 156,000 yen? I need ideas for next time." / "There's no next time."
- **Teaser:** Benny's card statement arrives. "What's a 'foreign transaction fee'?" / Maya: "…Next week."
