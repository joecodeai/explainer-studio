## PART B: THE FINAL SCRIPT, IN THE 7-PART FORMAT

### 1. VIDEO CONCEPT
- **Topic:** how insurance actually works, and who wins.
- **Argument:** insurance is one of humanity's best ideas. Everyone pays a little so the unlucky few don't lose everything. But it's a bet you're hoping to lose, and we're bad at choosing which bets to make. We overpay to avoid small bills, and we let companies profit from our loyalty and our inertia.
- **Comedic angle:** the "bucket" explains the whole industry by escalation: from a village bucket to a tower, with Gerald, a fox mascot and float. Then three people meet the bucket:
  - Ryan claims, and loses anyway.
  - Ryan insures a kettle.
  - Brayden runs his own bucket, and spends it.
- **Why it's interesting:** everyone pays for insurance, and almost nobody knows that insurers make much of their money by *investing your premiums while they wait*. Nor that economists have measured how irrationally we buy it.

[A crowded coffee house: men in wigs reading shipping lists over coffee.] 🔊 coffee-house chatter, cups

**MAYA**: Wealthy merchants would write their names under the description of a ship and its cargo, each taking a share of the risk. Which is where the word "underwriter" comes from.

[A quill signs name after name under a drawing of a ship.]

**MAYA**: That coffee house became Lloyd's of London. And in 1912, it had insured a ship called the Titanic, for a million pounds. Nearly five million dollars, back then.

(beat)

**MAYA**: It paid out in full, within thirty days.

◇OVER: Ryan holding up his phone; on screen a claim tracker reads "DAY 214" · **RYAN** *(quietly wounded)*: Thirty days? My phone claim's been open since March.

**MAYA**: Fire insurance also came from a disaster. After the Great Fire of 1666 destroyed most of the City of London, a builder called Nicholas Barbon opened the Fire Office, in 1680.

[Old London rebuilt; little metal plaques nailed above doors.]

**MAYA**: Insurers ran their own fire brigades and nailed metal badges, called fire marks, to the houses they covered. By 1690, one London house in ten was insured.

**MAYA** *(dry)*: You'll hear that the brigades just stood and watched uninsured houses burn. But fire doesn't check the badge. The house next door was usually somebody's customer.
