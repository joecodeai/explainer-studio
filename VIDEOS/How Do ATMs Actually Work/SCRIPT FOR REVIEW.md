- **Myth-bust:** typing your PIN backwards does NOT call the police (Inner Voice suggests it; Maya kills it).

- ATM robbery statistics: dark, but adds nothing.

## PART B: THE FINAL SCRIPT, IN THE 7-PART FORMAT

### 1. VIDEO CONCEPT
- **Topic:** what actually happens in the few seconds between your card going in and cash coming out, and who makes money from it.
- **Argument:** the ATM is a small miracle of global plumbing, and also a little box running old software that charges you nearly five dollars to borrow your own money.
- **Angle:** follow one twenty-dollar withdrawal. Ryan's request travels the world, Gerald approves it, and Brayden tries to turn the whole thing into passive income.
- **Why it's interesting:**
  - Everyone uses ATMs; almost nobody knows the machine doesn't know your balance.
  - The PIN length was chosen by one man's wife.
  - The first ATM was radioactive.
  - Antarctica has a spare-parts ATM.

### 2. FULL SCRIPT

#### COLD OPEN: THE WALL (0:00–0:40)
★MAYA · [Maya in her studio, sitting on the desk, facing camera] **MAYA** *(warm, conversational, a hint of wonder)*: You put a piece of plastic into a wall. You type four numbers. And the wall gives you money.

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(dry)*: If that happened once, we'd call it a miracle. Instead, we complain about the fee.

[A city street at night. Ryan at an ATM, staring at a screen with a spinning circle. Behind him in the queue, the fox, holding a tiny wallet.] 🔊 a faint ATM whirr, street noise

[Earlier: the fox at the machine ahead of Ryan, checking its balance, printing a receipt, then checking its balance again.]

**MAYA** *(dry)*: The person in front of you is always checking their balance. Twice.

◇OVER: Ryan at the ATM · **RYAN** *(patient, a little anxious)*: It's thinking.

**MAYA** *(gently correcting)*: It isn't thinking. It's asking.

♪ short sting · [Card: big **ATMs?** / small **HOW DO THEY ACTUALLY WORK**]

**MAYA**: So. How do ATMs actually work?

#### HOW WE GOT HERE: THE BATH (0:40–2:20)
★MAYA · **MAYA** *(storytelling)*: It started in a bath.

**MAYA**: In the 1960s, a Scottish engineer called John Shepherd-Barron was in the bath, thinking about how annoying it was that you could only get your own money when the bank was open.

[A man in a bath with a rubber duck, staring thoughtfully at the ceiling.]

**MAYA**: Then he thought of a chocolate machine. If a machine could give you a chocolate bar, why couldn't it give you cash?

[A vintage chocolate vending machine; a chocolate bar drops out, then a banknote.] 🔊 a clunk, then a soft "ding"

**MAYA**: On the twenty-seventh of June, 1967, the world's first cash machine opened at a Barclays bank in Enfield, north London. The first person to use it was a comedy actor called Reg Varney.

[A 1960s high street: a small crowd, a velvet curtain pulled back from a chunky machine in a bank wall.] 🔊 polite 1960s applause

**MAYA**: It gave out one-pound notes, up to a limit of ten pounds. About twenty-eight dollars at the time.

**MAYA**: Within days, Barclays had ordered six more machines. Then another fifty.

**MAYA**: America got its first one two years later, on the second of September, 1969, at Chemical Bank in Rockville Centre, New York. The newspaper ad said: "On Sept. 2, our bank will open at 9:00 and never close again."

[A 1969 newspaper advert held up by a delighted man in a hat, a cartoon bank wearing sunglasses.]

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(amused)*: Which, for a bank, was either a promise or a threat.

**MAYA**: There were no plastic cards yet. Instead, you used a special paper cheque, impregnated with carbon-14, which is mildly radioactive.

◇OVER: Ryan at his kitchen table, mug halfway to his mouth, frozen · **RYAN** *(slowly)*: The first cash machine was radioactive.

**MAYA** *(dry)*: Mildly.

**MAYA**: And then there was the number. Shepherd-Barron wanted a six-digit code, because he could remember his six-digit army number. So he checked with his wife, Caroline.

[A 1960s kitchen table: a woman with a cup of tea, shaking her head with a smile; a man holding up six fingers.]

**MAYA**: Over the kitchen table, she said she could only remember four.

(beat)

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(delighted, slightly incredulous)*: That's it. That's why your PIN is four digits. One woman, one cup of tea, the entire planet.

◇OVER: Ryan at the ATM · **RYAN** *(quietly, proud)*: Mine's my birthday.

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(instant, flat)*: Don't.

#### ABSURD SEQUENCE 1: THE JOURNEY (2:20–4:25)
**MAYA**: Here's the strange part. The machine doesn't know how much money you have.

[Ryan at the ATM, typing his PIN.] 🔊 four soft key beeps

**MAYA** *(explaining, quick)*: When you type your PIN, it's scrambled inside the keypad before it goes anywhere. Then the machine writes a tiny message: this card, this PIN, this much money, please.

[Ryan's request folds itself into a tiny paper plane and shoots out of the top of the ATM.] 🔊 a whoosh

▶MOTION · [The paper plane flies over rooftops, then a map, in a great arc.]

**MAYA**: That message goes to a network that connects thousands of banks, and the network passes it to your bank, wherever it is.

[A vast sorting office of tiny clerks catching paper planes and throwing them on. On the wall, a billboard: the fox in a headset, captioned with a thumbs-up.]

**MAYA**: And at your bank, someone says yes or no.

★LIPSYNC · [Gerald at his desk, a small headset, the sun drawing behind him, a paper plane landing gently in his hand] **GERALD** *(soft)*: Ryan. Nobody's in trouble. I've moved to Authorisations.

◇OVER: Gerald unfolding the plane, reading · **GERALD**: You'd like forty dollars. You have fourteen.

◇OVER: Ryan at the ATM · **RYAN**: Can I have fourteen, then?

◇OVER: Gerald, kindly · **GERALD**: Of course. (beat) The machine only does twenties.

[The ATM screen: AMOUNTS IN MULTIPLES OF $20.] (hold 1.0 s)

★LIPSYNC · **RYAN** *(a quiet realisation)*: So my money's in there. I just can't have it.

◇OVER: Gerald, brightening · **GERALD**: Good news, though. We can lend you the other six dollars.

◇OVER: Ryan · **RYAN** *(hopeful)*: Really?

◇OVER: Gerald, warmest smile · **GERALD**: Of course. There's a small overdraft fee. Twenty-six dollars and seventy-seven cents.

◇OVER: Ryan · **RYAN** *(very quietly)*: To borrow six dollars.

◇OVER: Gerald · **GERALD** *(helpful)*: Would you like a receipt? It'll say fourteen dollars.

◇OVER: Ryan · **RYAN**: I know it's fourteen dollars.

◇OVER: Gerald · **GERALD** *(gently)*: Most people like to check twice.

**MAYA**: That's not a joke number. In 2025, Bankrate put the average overdraft fee at twenty-six dollars and seventy-seven cents.

**MAYA**: All of that, there and back, usually takes a few seconds. Then the machine counts out notes from locked boxes inside it, called cassettes, and checks they haven't stuck together.

[Inside the ATM, a cutaway: cassettes of notes, rollers counting, a tiny sensor eye blinking.] 🔊 a soft mechanical shuffle

**MAYA**: And then there's the fee.

**MAYA**: In 2025, Bankrate found the average cost of using an ATM that isn't your bank's was four dollars and eighty-six cents. That's the machine's own charge, plus a separate charge from your own bank for going somewhere else.

[Card: big **$4.86** / small **TO USE SOMEONE ELSE'S ATM · US AVERAGE 2025**]

**MAYA**: When Bankrate started tracking it in 1998, the same thing cost a dollar ninety-seven. Do it once a week now, and it's about two hundred and fifty dollars a year.

◇OVER: Ryan at the ATM holding one twenty-dollar bill · **RYAN** *(doing the maths)*: So to take out twenty dollars, I pay nearly five.

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(dry)*: It's the only shop where you pay to collect your own shopping.

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(conversational anecdote, a little embarrassed)*: I once used an ATM abroad, panicked about the exchange rate, and took out what turned out to be about four dollars. The fee was more than the money.

[Maya's hand (no face) holding a single crumpled foreign banknote and a much longer receipt.] (hold 1.0 s)

#### WHO OWNS THE WALL (4:25–5:15)
**MAYA**: And a lot of those machines don't belong to banks at all. Shops, bars and private owners can buy an ATM, fill it with cash, set a fee, and keep it.

[A bar with a lonely ATM next to the toilets, glowing.] 🔊 bar chatter

**MAYA** *(fair)*: To be fair to them, an ATM isn't free to run. Someone has to buy the machine, fill it with cash, pay for the connection, and send an armoured truck when it's empty.

[An armoured truck parked outside a corner shop; a guard carrying a cash cassette, the fox watching from a bus stop.] 🔊 a heavy van door

**MAYA**: There are somewhere around half a million ATMs in the United States, and about three million around the world.

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(genuinely delighted)*: There are even two in Antarctica. At McMurdo Station, run by Wells Fargo. Only one of them works at a time.

**MAYA**: The other one is spare parts.

▶MOTION · [A snowy research station, an ATM inside a hut; outside the window, the fox in a tiny parka, waiting patiently in a queue of one.] 🔊 howling wind

#### ABSURD SEQUENCE 2: BRAYDEN'S PASSIVE INCOME (5:15–6:50)
**MAYA**: Which gave Brayden an idea.

★LIPSYNC · [Brayden in his bare white flat, standing proudly next to a brand-new ATM] **BRAYDEN** *(loud, thrilled)*: Bro. I own an ATM now. Passive income.

**MAYA**: He set the fee at nine ninety-nine.

[The ATM screen: FEE $9.99.] 🔊 a cash register "ding"

**MAYA**: He installed it in his flat.

[Wide: a completely empty white flat, one bare bulb, the ATM glowing in the corner. Nobody comes.] (silence 1.5 s)

**MAYA**: Nobody came.

**MAYA**: Then, one evening, a customer.

[Ryan standing awkwardly in Brayden's empty flat, in front of the ATM.]

◇OVER: Ryan at Brayden's ATM · **RYAN** *(polite)*: I just needed twenty dollars for a pizza.

**MAYA**: Ryan paid nine ninety-nine to Brayden, and a dollar sixty-four to his own bank, for a twenty-dollar pizza.

◇OVER: Brayden, hand on Ryan's shoulder, beaming · **BRAYDEN**: You're investing in me, bro.

**MAYA**: He never came back.

**MAYA**: Then Brayden found out something about owning an ATM. It doesn't come with money. You have to fill it yourself.

[Brayden feeding his own crumpled notes into the open back of the ATM, one by one.]

◇OVER: Brayden at his ATM · **BRAYDEN** *(confident)*: So I'm the bank now.

**MAYA**: The machine ran out by Wednesday. So he paid an armoured truck to bring his own money back to his own flat.

[An armoured truck squeezed into a narrow residential street; two guards carrying one small cash cassette up Brayden's stairs, Brayden holding the door open.] 🔊 a heavy van door, footsteps on stairs

**MAYA**: Then his rent was due, so he took his money out again.

[Brayden at the front of his own ATM, typing his PIN.] 🔊 key beeps

**MAYA** *(precise)*: The machine charged him nine ninety-nine, which went to him. And his own bank charged him a dollar sixty-four for using an ATM that wasn't theirs.

◇OVER: Brayden counting a few notes, beaming · **BRAYDEN**: Every time I take my money out, I make ten bucks.

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(flat)*: He doesn't. He loses a dollar sixty-four.

(beat)

★LIPSYNC · **BRAYDEN** *(serene)*: Bro, I'm vertically integrated.

#### THE DARK SIDE (6:50–8:10)
**MAYA**: Not everyone at an ATM is there to take out their own money.

[A close-up of a normal-looking ATM card slot; then the front lifts off like a mask, revealing a hidden card reader and a tiny pinhole camera.] 🔊 a soft plastic click

**MAYA**: Skimmers are fake card readers that fit over the real one, often with a hidden camera to watch your PIN. The FBI estimates skimming costs banks and customers more than a billion dollars a year.

**MAYA**: So, cover the keypad when you type. And if anything on the machine looks loose, or a bit different, use a different machine.

[A hand typing a PIN while the other hand covers the keypad like a little roof.]

**MAYA**: Then there's jackpotting. In 2010, a security researcher called Barnaby Jack stood on stage at the Black Hat hacking conference and made two ATMs spit out cash, live, in front of an audience.

▶MOTION · [On a stage under spotlights, an ATM showering banknotes into the air; a small audience gasps.] 🔊 a crowd gasp, then cheering

**MAYA**: He did it to show the manufacturers how, so they'd fix it. Criminals have since used similar tricks for real.

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(conspiratorial)*: Part of the problem was the software. In 2014, when Microsoft stopped supporting Windows XP, an estimated ninety-five percent of the world's ATMs were still running it.

◇OVER: Ryan at the ATM, squinting at it suspiciously · **RYAN**: The cash machine is older than my phone.

◇OVER: Ryan close-up, a tiny dark imp on his shoulder · **INNER VOICE** *(whisper)*: Type your PIN backwards. The police will come.

**MAYA**: They won't. That's a myth. Fact-checkers have looked into it more than once: entering your PIN backwards doesn't summon anyone.

◇OVER: Ryan, slightly disappointed · **RYAN** *(quietly)*: I was going to do it for fun.

#### THE VERDICT AND THE REVEAL (8:10–9:10)
**MAYA** *(sincere)*: Fewer of us use cash every year. In 2025, the Federal Reserve found cash made up just fourteen percent of payments in the US. But eighty-one percent of people had still used cash in the past month.

**MAYA**: Cash is the thing we keep for when everything else goes wrong. And the ATM is how we get it.

★MAYA · **MAYA**: A machine on your street asks a bank that might be thousands of miles away whether you're good for forty dollars. And it usually gets an answer before you've finished putting your card away.

**MAYA**: More and more machines don't even need the card any more. You can tap your phone instead, and the same message makes the same journey.

**MAYA**: So use your own bank's machines when you can. In the US, a machine that charges a fee has to tell you how much before you're committed, so read that screen and feel free to cancel. Cover the keypad. Pick a PIN that isn't your birthday.

◇OVER: Ryan at the ATM · **RYAN** *(sincere)*: I'm changing it.

**MAYA**: And if you've ever wondered whether there's a tiny person inside the machine, counting out your notes…

[The ATM from the outside, quiet, a single twenty-dollar bill sliding out.]

**MAYA** *(deadpan)*: There isn't.

[Cutaway inside the ATM: the fox in a green banker's visor, counting notes by lamplight, licking its thumb.] (hold 1.5 s)

★MAYA · **MAYA** *(tiny shrug)*: Usually.

(silence 1.5 s) · END
