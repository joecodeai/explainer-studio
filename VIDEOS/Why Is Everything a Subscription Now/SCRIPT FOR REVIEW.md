### A3. Pre-flight checks

- **The three absurd sequences.** Each starts from a recognisable premise, escalates with characters interacting, and lands on a payoff:
  - **Future Ryan:** optimism bias → he uses everything → meets someone → Lisbon → air fryer.
  - **Retention:** a polite request → reframing → a pause offer that is just the status quo → a hostage sun → death still needs an in-person visit → four rooms → Monday.
  - **Brayden Owns Nothing:** the door is premium → no sofa → the fridge light is paused → sitting is on Plus → the floor is metered → he sells himself → he refuses a gift.
- **Maya's registers:**
  - deadpan (BMW)
  - fair (music)
  - personal (meditation app)
  - curious ("So who is signing us up for all this?")
  - nearly laughing ("Ryan's gym")
  - irritated ("That's not what he said.")
  - quiet disbelief (the Iliad)
  - serious (the FTC's "stop them")
  - blunt (the verdict)
- **Plain lines with no joke on purpose:** C+R, the gym figures, Amazon's settlement, the click-to-cancel history and Instant Ink.
- **Ryan is never sarcastic.** Every line is honest, polite or worried. His big ridiculous moment is Future Ryan; his small ones are apologising to the subscriptions and the investment line.
- **The fox** appears four times, is never mentioned, and escalates (see Part 4).
- **Banned patterns** (HUMOR_GUIDE §9): none present. There's no "not ideal", no "things got worse", no "step one/step two" and no "X was not impressed".

## PART B: THE FINAL SCRIPT, IN THE 7-PART FORMAT

### 1. VIDEO CONCEPT
- **Topic:** why so much of modern life (software, cars, printers, gyms, even heat) is now paid monthly instead of bought once.
- **Central argument:** some subscriptions really are better. But the model mostly works because of three human weaknesses:
  1. We forget what we pay for (C+R survey).
  2. We sign up for a more disciplined future self (the gym study).
  3. Leaving is made just annoying enough to put off (FTC v. Amazon, the Adobe case).

  The newest twist is charging monthly for things you've already bought (BMW's seats, HP Instant Ink).
- **Comedic angle:**
  - **Ryan** is the polite man paying for a life someone else is living: Future Ryan.
  - **Gerald** is the calm voice of the exit that never quite opens.
  - **Brayden** takes "own nothing and be happy" as a personal challenge.
  - **Maya** is the friend who knows exactly how much you're paying.
- **Why it's interesting:**
  - Everyone has a subscription they forgot about.
  - The real facts (a seat heater behind a paywall, a cancellation flow named after a ten-year war, compensation paid in free Adobe) are stranger than satire.
  - It continues the previous video's best-rated running gag without repeating it.

### 2. FULL SCRIPT

**Markers:**

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| [ ] | Animation: a still keyframe with a slow camera move unless marked ▶MOTION |
| 👁 | Unmentioned background gag |
| ⏱ | Animation-only hold, counted in the runtime |
| 🔊 | Sound effect |
| ♪ | Sting (none used; see Part 4) |
| (beat) | Short pause between lines or sentences, never mid-sentence |
| (silence Xs) | Deliberate silence |
| ★LIPSYNC | Lip-synced line: the speaker's face is visible (max 10) |
| ◇OVER: … | The line plays over what's described (a listener's reaction, an object or a cutaway); no lip-sync |
| ▶MOTION | Animated-motion shot, one simple action (max 6) |
| Card | A text or number card made in the edit. No readable text inside any keyframe. |

Maya is never on screen. The Inner Voice is a tiny impish shadow creature on Ryan's shoulder, and its lines are always ◇OVER it, never lip-synced. Section timestamps use the bible's per-character speeds (see Part 5).
