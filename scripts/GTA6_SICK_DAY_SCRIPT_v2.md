# Ryan Figures It Out — The Foolproof Plan to Call In Sick for GTA 6
### Rewrite v2: diagnosis, direction, episode, reel, assessment

---

## A. DIAGNOSIS OF THE CURRENT DRAFT

**Strongest material (protected):**
- **Scene 1 as a whole:** "Yes. But that's for holidays." / "Is a medical situation." / "Not yet. There's still time." This is the premise, the character and the tone in nine lines.
- **Dana's escalation in the call:** presentation → budget → client notes. It's the best structural joke in the episode: work reaching into his sick day.
- "I'm beginning to regret being such a good employee." / "Fever. I'm delirious."
- "So the game called in sick. Twice." It's a real parallel, not a pun.
- "I've accidentally been promoted by the flu." / "Apparently, I'm the only person in the country who isn't sick."
- "That's between me and capitalism."
- **The idea of Maya's annual leave reveal.** The execution needed work.

**Weakest lines (cut or replaced):**
- **"I'm incubating", "It's a very advanced illness", "It broke. It's a miracle."** Each one tells you the joke instead of letting you find it.
- **"Double standards", "Full circle", "Details".** Two-word buttons that exist only to end a beat.
- **"Dana once asked me for evidence that I'd read an email."** It's a constructed joke-line, not something a person says.
- **"That's how I do my expenses."** Ryan admits to fraud for a laugh; it breaks the character, who isn't a cheat, just a rationaliser.
- **Maya's V.O. paragraphs (scenes 3, 5, 7, 8).** She turns into a documentary narrator. That's the "documentary interrupting a comedy" problem.

**Repetitive pattern:**
- Almost every exchange runs: Ryan says something absurd → Maya corrects him logically → Ryan tops it.
- That pattern repeats 20+ times across 13 scenes, so by Scene 6 the audience can predict the shape of every beat.
- Maya also ends too many scenes on a correction.

**Too exposition-heavy:** scenes 3, 5, 6 and 7 each stop the story to deliver facts, and Ryan's plan doesn't move in any of them.

**Inconsistencies:**
- Ryan is supposed to send Dana the deck, but it's never established that he didn't.
- Maya's whereabouts on Thursday morning aren't set up, so her final reveal arrives from nowhere instead of being sitting in plain sight.
- "I'm never unwell" made Dana's absence too telegraphed.

**Visual comedy left unused:**
- The download bar, the countdown, his documents and the chat order.
- The meeting tiles, Maya's pyjamas on a work day, and the empty office in silence.

---

## B. COMEDY DIRECTION

**Identity:**
- A small, dry office comedy about a clever man applying all his professional skills to avoiding his profession.
- Ryan treats his fake sick day like a corporate project, with a business case, risk assessment, compliance research, precedent and contingency planning. He is better at faking a day off than at his actual job.
- **The villain is the office itself.** Work finds him at home, follows him to the office and promotes him for showing up.

**The central irony (never stated by any character):** Ryan spends more effort avoiding one day of work than it takes to book one.
- The audience should feel it building from Scene 3 onward.
- Maya should never explain it. The final scene just puts the answer on screen: a form, and a controller.

**Running devices (they carry the comedy so the dialogue doesn't have to):**
1. **Two files on Ryan's desktop:** `Q4_Client_Deck_v2.pptx — last edited Monday` and `WHY_THURSDAY.pptx — last edited 2 minutes ago`. He has built a presentation to justify his sick day instead of finishing the one he's paid for. It pays off when Dana asks for the deck.
2. **The download bar** is the episode's clock and its counterpoint:
   - it's crawling while he works;
   - its time remaining goes up;
   - it finishes when he can't play.
3. **Corporate language applied to faking illness:** "options", "regulatory", "direction of travel", "reinvested", "take it offline". Deadpan, never winked at.
4. **Maya, hiding in plain sight:**
   - Thursday morning she's in pyjamas at 10:00 on a weekday, unhurried, never explained.
   - Ryan is too busy to notice; the audience should.
   - The last scene confirms what they suspected.

**Character voices:**
- **Ryan:** articulate, earnest, never winks, occasionally genuinely right, but always a step past where he should have stopped.
- **Maya:** short questions, long looks, and a lot of letting him talk. She corrects him rarely, and when she does it's in four words or fewer. She also has her own life going on: books, coffee, a toothbrush.
- **Dana:** a decent, busy manager. Every line she has is reasonable. The cruelty is only in the timing.

**Structure:** each scene moves Ryan's *plan* forward one stage, and the facts arrive as his research for that stage. Nobody stops to explain.

## C. THE YOUTUBE EPISODE (~8–9 minutes)

### SCENE 1 — THE BUSINESS PLAN
*Wednesday evening. Ryan's flat. Ryan at the kitchen table, laptop open. In the corner of the screen, a countdown: GTA VI — 14:22:07. Maya on the sofa with a book.*

**RYAN:** I've been looking into taking tomorrow off.
**MAYA** *(not looking up)*: You have annual leave.
**RYAN:** Yes. But that's for holidays.
*Maya turns a page.*
**MAYA:** And tomorrow?
**RYAN:** Is a medical situation.
**MAYA:** You're not ill.
**RYAN:** Not yet. *(beat)* There's still time.
*Maya lowers the book. Ryan keeps typing.*
**MAYA:** You're planning to develop a medical condition.
**RYAN:** I'm exploring my options.
*She watches him a moment longer. Then lifts the book again.*
**MAYA:** Let me know what you come down with.

### TITLE CARD
*Music sting. On screen:*
**GRAND THEFT AUTO VI — OUT THURSDAY, 19 NOVEMBER.**
**The first new GTA in thirteen years.**
*Then:* **RYAN FIGURES IT OUT**

### SCENE 2 — EARLIER THAT DAY
*Wednesday afternoon. The office. Dana types fast, headset round her neck. Ryan arrives holding a mug he clearly doesn't need.*

**RYAN:** Busy?
**DANA** *(eyes on screen)*: Yes.
**RYAN:** Me too. Very. *(beat)* Anything big tomorrow?
**DANA:** The ten o'clock. You're presenting.
**RYAN:** The ten o'clock. Right. *(beat)* And you'll be... here. For that.
**DANA** *(looks up)*: Where else would I be?
**RYAN:** Nowhere. No. It's just — people get ill. This time of year.
**DANA:** Do they?
**RYAN:** Apparently. Very suddenly. I read about it.
*Dana looks at him for slightly too long.*
**DANA:** Send me the deck tonight.
**RYAN:** Tonight. Absolutely.
*He leaves. Dana goes back to typing. On her second monitor, unnoticed, a browser tab:* **GTA VI — Pre-load complete.**

### SCENE 3 — THE BUSINESS CASE
*The flat, that evening. Ryan's laptop desktop, close: two files.*
`Q4_Client_Deck_v2.pptx — last edited Monday`
`WHY_THURSDAY.pptx — last edited 2 minutes ago`
*Ryan turns the laptop toward Maya. She did not ask for this.*

**RYAN:** Context.
*Slide 1 — big type:* **13 YEARS.**
**RYAN:** The last one came out in 2013. I was fifteen.
**MAYA:** And you've been waiting since.
**RYAN:** I've been patient since.
*Slide 2:* **GTA V — ~230 MILLION COPIES. 3rd best-selling game ever.**
**RYAN:** Two hundred and thirty million copies. Take-Two says it made a billion dollars faster than any entertainment product in history. *(beat)* Any. Films. Albums. Anything.
*Slide 3:* **GTA VI — $1BN+ IN PRE-ORDERS (Sensor Tower estimate)**
**RYAN:** The new one's done over a billion in pre-orders. Before anyone's even played it.
**MAYA:** Including you.
**RYAN:** *(small pause)* Including me.
*Slide 4: a newspaper headline —* **"Ten years, thousands of staff and a billion dollars" — The Guardian.**
**RYAN:** Ten years. Thousands of people. A billion dollars.
**MAYA:** And you want one Thursday.
**RYAN:** When you put it like that, it's the least I can do.
*Maya looks at the slide. Then at the other file, the one last edited on Monday. She says nothing.*
**MAYA:** How much was it?
**RYAN:** Seventy-nine ninety-nine. The last one was sixty, in 2013 — that's about eighty-five in today's money. So technically, I've saved money.
**MAYA:** You bought the ninety-nine ninety-nine one.
**RYAN:** I saved on the standard edition. And reinvested.

### SCENE 4 — RISK ASSESSMENT
*Later. A notepad beside the laptop with a heading in capitals: RISKS. One item so far.*

**RYAN:** Problem.
*Maya keeps reading.*
**RYAN:** Australia's Fair Work Ombudsman says an employer can ask for evidence. Even for one day off sick.
**MAYA:** We don't live in Australia.
**RYAN:** No. But it shows the direction of travel.
**MAYA:** Where's it travelling?
**RYAN:** Towards evidence. *(beat)* Which I don't have. Because I'm well. That's the whole problem with being well.
*He scrolls. Brightens.*
**RYAN:** On the other hand. Back in April, the head of Take-Two — the company that makes it — joked that a lot of people would be calling in sick on the nineteenth.
**MAYA:** The man selling you the game.
**RYAN:** He's acknowledged the condition.
**MAYA:** He's also got your eighty dollars.
**RYAN:** That's between me and capitalism.
*On the notepad, under RISKS, he writes:* **Industry-recognised condition.** *He moves it to a new column, headed:* **STRENGTHS.**

### SCENE 5 — THE DELAYS
*Maya picks up the notepad to put it out of reach. Underneath it is an older page. Two lines, both crossed out:*
~~**Autumn 2025 — book flu**~~
~~**26 May 2026 — book flu**~~
*She looks at it. Then at him.*

**MAYA:** You've done this before.
**RYAN:** Twice.
**MAYA:** "Book flu."
**RYAN:** It was meant to come out last autumn. Then they moved it to May. Then they moved it to November. Every time, I had a plan.
**MAYA:** And every time—
**RYAN:** Rockstar apologised. Very gracious about it. "Sorry for adding additional time."
*Beat.*
**RYAN:** When you think about it, the game called in sick. Twice.
**MAYA:** And nobody asked it for evidence.
**RYAN** *(quietly, with feeling)*: Nobody asked it for evidence.

### SCENE 6 — THE PAPERWORK
*Nearly midnight. Maya comes back from the kitchen with tea. Ryan is at the table with printed pages, a highlighter and the company staff handbook — bristling with coloured sticky tabs.*

**MAYA:** Have you printed the internet?
**RYAN:** You can't highlight a website.
*He holds up a page.*
**RYAN:** In the UK you can self-certify for up to seven days. No fit note.
**MAYA:** Seven days.
**RYAN:** I only need one. I'm not greedy.
**MAYA:** And America?
**RYAN:** No national law. Most people in private-sector jobs don't get paid sick days unless their state or their employer gives them. So it depends where you live, and who you work for.
*He taps the handbook.*
**RYAN:** Which is why I've read this.
**MAYA:** You've read the staff handbook.
**RYAN:** Twice.
**MAYA:** How long did that take?
**RYAN** *(checks his watch)*: About three hours.
*Maya nods slowly. Takes a sip of tea. Doesn't say anything. Goes back to the sofa.*

### SCENE 7 — THE EPIDEMIC
*Ryan, energised, scrolling news on his phone.*

**RYAN:** Good news. I'm not alone.
**MAYA:** That's rarely good news.
**RYAN:** When the last one came out in 2013, so many people suddenly got ill it got a name. GTA flu.
**MAYA:** Catchy.
**RYAN:** And a survey here says nine hundred thousand people in Britain are going to pretend to be sick tomorrow.
**MAYA:** How many people did they ask?
**RYAN:** *(scrolls; beat)* A thousand.
**MAYA:** So nine hundred people said yes, and someone added some zeros.
**RYAN:** Still. Nine hundred people. That's a movement.
*He scrolls further.*
**RYAN:** There's a figure here saying it'll cost the American economy a billion dollars.
**MAYA:** Says who?
**RYAN:** *(reading)* It all goes back to one article from a Spanish broadcaster, last summer. And the Guardian says the maths was... *(reads)* "back-of-the-napkin."
**MAYA:** So the economy will be fine.
**RYAN:** *(genuinely a little disappointed)* Shame. I liked being a threat to the economy.

### SCENE 8 — PRECEDENT
*Ryan's bedroom, lights off. Ryan in bed, face lit by his phone. Maya in the doorway, brushing her teeth.*

**RYAN:** In Japan, in the nineties, a game called Dragon Quest had so many kids skipping school and so many adults calling in sick that they moved the release to Saturdays.
**MAYA** *(toothbrush)*: Mm.
**RYAN:** A whole country rearranged itself around a game.
**MAYA** *(takes the toothbrush out)*: Or they rearranged the game around the country.
*Ryan thinks about this.*
**RYAN:** Rockstar didn't do that.
**MAYA:** Rockstar picked a Thursday.
**RYAN:** It's a test.
**MAYA:** Goodnight, Ryan.
*She switches off the hall light. In the dark, his phone shows the alarm he's just set:*
**6:00 — CALL DANA (sound ill)**

### SCENE 9 — THE CALL
*Thursday, 6:01. Ryan sits up in bed in a hoodie, duvet arranged around him, a box of tissues and a glass of water placed carefully on the bedside table — for a phone call nobody can see. The laptop beside him:* **GTA VI — Downloading 3% · 11 hrs remaining.**

**RYAN** *(clears throat; rough voice)*: Hi, Dana. It's Ryan.
**DANA** *(V.O., keyboard clatter)*: Ryan. Hi.
**RYAN:** Just wanted to let you know I'm feeling a little under the weather.
**DANA:** Oh no. What is it?
**RYAN:** Flu, I think. Headache. Fatigue. *(beat)* Very sudden.
*The download ticks over to 4%.*
**DANA:** Right. Rest up. Anything urgent you need to hand over?
*Ryan looks at the laptop.*
**RYAN:** Nothing that can't wait.
**DANA:** Great. Actually, could you just send the deck over before you log off? For the ten o'clock.
*Ryan closes his eyes. The deck was last edited on Monday.*
**RYAN:** Of course.
**DANA:** And the revised budget.
**RYAN:** Naturally.
**DANA:** And the client notes. And if you could update the tracker, that'd be amazing.
*Slowly, Ryan pulls the laptop closer. Minimises the download. Opens a spreadsheet.*
**RYAN:** I'm beginning to regret being such a good employee.
**DANA:** Sorry?
**RYAN** *(coughs)*: Fever. I'm delirious.
**DANA:** Feel better.
*Click. Ryan, alone, opens Q4_Client_Deck_v2. It's half finished.*

### SCENE 10 — WORKING FROM BED
*Thursday, 9:20. The kitchen table. Ryan in the duvet, typing fast. The work chat pings every few seconds. Maya wanders past — pyjamas, slippers, coffee, in no hurry at all.*

**MAYA:** How's the flu?
**RYAN:** Productive.
*Ping.*
**RYAN** *(reading)*: "Thanks, Ryan. One more thing." *(beat)* There's always one more thing. There's never one fewer thing.
*He glances at the download window:* **14% · 6 hrs remaining.** *As he watches, it changes to* **7 hrs remaining.**
**RYAN:** It went up.
**MAYA:** What did?
**RYAN:** The time. I've been ill all morning and it's further away than when I started.
*Ping. A new message from Dana:* **"Can you dial into the 10? Camera on if you're up to it"**
*Ryan reads it twice.*
**RYAN:** If I dial in, I have to be ill. For an hour. On camera.
*Long pause. He looks at the deck, the budget, the chat, the download.*
**RYAN:** It'd be less work to go in.
*He closes the laptop and stands, duvet still on his shoulders. Maya sips her coffee.*
**MAYA:** What do I tell people?
**RYAN:** That I made a full recovery.
*He leaves. Maya puts her feet up on the coffee table, picks up the TV remote, and settles in. It's 9:24 on a Thursday.*

### SCENE 11 — THE REALITY
*9:58. The office. The lift doors open on Ryan.*
*Silence.*
*Rows of empty desks. Chairs pushed in. One monitor running a screensaver. The printer blinks.*
*Ryan walks to his desk slowly. Sits. Two full seconds of nothing.*
*His phone buzzes. The team chat, in order:*
- **Ryan (6:02):** Feeling under the weather today, will be mostly offline.
- **Dana (7:02):** Not feeling great. Taking a sick day.
- **Priya (7:15):** Same, sorry all.
- **Tom (7:31):** Woke up with a fever.
- **Leo (7:48):** Working from home today, might be patchy.

*Ryan reads the whole thing. Scrolls back to the top, to his own message, the first one. Beat.*

**RYAN:** Interesting.

*An email arrives:* **From: Dana · Subject: Quick favour**
*"Ryan — just saw you badged in, so glad you're feeling better. Since you're the only one in, could you chair the 10am? Thanks for stepping up."*
*A second email, immediately:* **Subject: Acting Team Lead (effective today)**

*Ryan looks at it. Then around the empty office. Then back.*

**RYAN** *(quietly)*: I've accidentally been promoted by the flu.

*He opens the calendar.* **Nine meetings. Three deadlines. One client presentation, at 2pm.** *It's the deck.*
*His phone buzzes:* **MAYA:** How's your day off?
*He types:*
**RYAN:** Apparently, I'm the only person in the country who isn't sick.
*Maya's typing dots appear. They stop. Nothing comes.*
*Another notification slides down over the chat:* **GTA VI is ready to play.**
*He looks at it for a long moment. Then at the calendar reminder:* **Team Sync — starting now (you are the host).**
*He clicks Join.*

**RYAN:** Well. At least someone's getting paid to suffer.

### SCENE 12 — THE 10 AM MEETING
*A glass meeting room. Ryan alone at the end of a table for twelve. On the laptop: a video call of black tiles, cameras off. Dana. Priya. Tom. Leo.*

**RYAN:** Morning, everyone. Thanks for joining.
*Silence. A thumbs-up reaction floats up from Priya's tile. Ryan nods at it.*
**RYAN:** Let's go round with updates. Priya?
*Nothing. Priya's tile shows a small "Away" clock.*
**RYAN:** Tom?
**TOM** *(muffled, unmuting)*: Yeah, nothing from me, mate.
*Behind his voice, faintly: an engine, revving hard. His tile mutes.*
**RYAN:** Thanks, Tom. Get well soon.
*Dana's tile switches to* **"Dana is presenting".** *For half a second the shared screen shows the GTA VI main menu. Then:* **"Dana stopped sharing."**
*Ryan looks into his webcam. A long, perfectly neutral pause.*
**RYAN:** Lovely. Let's take the rest of the agenda offline.
*He ends the call, sits back and looks out at forty empty desks.*

### SCENE 13 — THE ANNUAL LEAVE
*Evening. Ryan lets himself into the flat, tie loose, carrying the printed client presentation, which he delivered alone. The sound of the game fills the flat: a car chase.*
*Maya is on the sofa, feet up, controller in hand, snacks laid out. GTA VI is on the TV. She's clearly been there for a while.*
*Ryan stops in the doorway.*

**RYAN:** You're playing it.
**MAYA** *(eyes on the screen)*: Mm.
**RYAN:** You're not at work.
**MAYA:** I'm on annual leave.
*Pause.*
**RYAN:** Since when?
**MAYA:** September. I booked it the day they announced the date.
*Ryan processes this.*
**RYAN:** It wasn't sick leave.
**MAYA:** No. That's for when you're ill.
*He stands there. Then sits down beside her, very slowly. Without looking away from the screen, she passes him the second controller. He doesn't take it. He takes out his phone, opens the HR portal and starts filling in the annual leave form.*
**RYAN:** How do you—
**MAYA:** There's a form.
**RYAN** *(typing)*: "Reason for leave." *(beat; types)* "Holiday."
*Submit. Instantly, ping:*
**"Request declined. You are the only available cover for: Acting Team Lead."**
*Ryan looks at the screen. Maya un-pauses the game. Sirens.*
**RYAN** *(very quietly)*: I think I'm coming down with something.
**MAYA:** There's still time.

### END CARD
**MAYA (V.O.):** Ryan Figures It Out. If you're watching this at work today... get well soon.

## E. EDITORIAL ASSESSMENT

**The five most important improvements:**
1. **The story is now the joke.**
   - Every scene advances Ryan's plan one stage: business case → risk assessment → delays → paperwork → epidemic → precedent → execution → failure.
   - The facts arrive as his research for each stage, so the documentary voice-overs are gone.
2. **The central irony runs underneath without being said.**
   - He builds a presentation for his sick day instead of the one he's paid for, reads the handbook twice, and spends three hours on paperwork.
   - Nobody points it out. The two-file desktop, Maya's silent nod and the final form do it.
3. **Maya is a person, not a corrector.**
   - Her lines are short questions and long looks, and she has her own evening (book, tea, toothbrush).
   - Her reveal is planted in plain sight: pyjamas at 9:24 on a Thursday, feet up as he leaves. The audience gets there before Ryan.
4. **The office is the villain.**
   - Dana is never unkind, just reasonable at the worst moments.
   - The system declines his leave because he showed up, so work punishes him for doing the right thing.
5. **A circular ending.**
   - It closes on the opening's logic: "I think I'm coming down with something." / "There's still time."
   - It's inevitable in hindsight and needs no moral.

**The strongest new comedic moments:**
- The two files: **`WHY_THURSDAY.pptx` — 2 minutes ago** vs **`Q4_Client_Deck` — Monday**.
- "When you put it like that, it's the least I can do."
- **The crossed-out "Book flu" plans** from both previous delays, discovered by Maya (a visual that does the joke).
- Moving "Industry-recognised condition" from RISKS to STRENGTHS.
- "Nobody asked it for evidence." Ryan's quiet, genuine grievance.
- "Shame. I liked being a threat to the economy."
- **The download's time remaining going UP** after a morning of work.
- **"It'd be less work to go in."** Ryan stumbles into the truth for the wrong reason.
- **The chat order:** his own sick message is the first one, at 6:02.
- Maya's typing dots appearing, then stopping.
- **Dana sharing her screen:** the GTA menu for half a second. Then "Let's take the rest of the agenda offline."
- **"Request declined. You are the only available cover."**

**Scenes that could still be shortened:**
- **Scenes 6 and 7** are the softest middle; if the edit runs long, merge them into one "research" scene.
- **Scene 8 (Dragon Quest)** can lose its first two lines and still land the transition.
- **Scene 2** can lose "Do they?" if the pacing drags.

**Lines to deliver completely deadpan (zero smile, zero emphasis):**
- "Not yet. There's still time."
- "Is a medical situation."
- "I'm exploring my options."
- "It's the least I can do."
- "I saved on the standard edition. And reinvested."
- "That's the whole problem with being well."
- "Nobody asked it for evidence."
- "Interesting."
- "I've accidentally been promoted by the flu."
- "Let's take the rest of the agenda offline."
- **Maya's "There's still time."** It must be flat. If she smiles, it dies.

**The visual moments most likely to get the biggest laughs:**
1. The empty office in silence, then the chat with Ryan's 6:02 message at the top.
2. Dana's half-second GTA screen-share in the meeting.
3. The download's time remaining going up.
4. The tissues and water arranged for a phone call.
5. "Request declined. You are the only available cover."
6. The crossed-out "Book flu" plans.
