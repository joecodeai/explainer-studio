## The new picks

### 1. The Man Who Invented the Olympics Secretly Entered and Won Gold
**Alt hook:** *"For 36 years, the Olympics gave gold medals for painting."*

- **Story:**
  - From 1912 to 1948, the Olympics held real medal competitions in architecture, sculpture, painting, music and literature. Every entry had to be about sport.
  - At Stockholm 1912, local artists barely cared. So Baron Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Olympics and IOC president, organised the art contest himself.
  - The literature category drew 7 entries. The winner was "Ode to Sport", by "Georges Hohrod and M. Eschbach". That was Coubertin, under two fake names. He revealed it years later.
  - The art events died after 1948. Artists were selling their work, which broke the amateur rules. The IOC no longer even counts those medals.
- **Ryan & Maya:** Ryan organises the office bake-off, enters under a fake name and judges it. Maya: "That's not cheating, Ryan. That's the Olympic tradition."
- **Why it clicks:** the title is a scandal with a founder villain, plus an "Olympics had art medals?!" fact most people have never heard.
- **Coverage:** **open lane**. Only 2 small videos.
- **Sources:**
  - ✅ Olympics.com, "World Poetry Day: when busting a rhyme was worth a gold medal" (2025)
  - ✅ Wikipedia, Pierre de Coubertin

### 2. This Island's Post Office Was the Ocean
**Alt hook:** *"They mailed letters by throwing them into the sea. It worked."*

- **Story:**
  - St Kilda is the most remote inhabited island group in the UK, with no regular mail.
  - In 1877, a stranded journalist, John Sands, carved a tiny wooden boat, put a letter inside and launched it into the Atlantic. It landed in Orkney 9 days later, and a rescue boat came for him and 9 shipwrecked Austrian sailors.
  - In 1885 a storm wrecked the islanders' food stores. A 14-year-old, Alexander Ferguson, launched 5 mailboats asking for help. One reached Lewis, the story made the papers, £110 was raised and a supply boat was chartered.
  - The islanders kept "posting" this way: a carved boat with a float made from an inflated sheep's bladder.
  - The twist ending: in 2010 the National Trust launched a replica with 7 postcards inside. In 2020, four kids found it on a beach in Arctic Norway, 1,000+ miles away, and the cards were still dry. The cards were delivered, including one to Prince Charles, who wrote back.
- **Ryan & Maya:** Ryan's parcel is "out for delivery" for 9 days. He builds a sheep-bladder boat to return it. Maya tracks it on a map.
- **Why it clicks:** it's a survival story with a hopeful ending and a modern payoff (the 10-year delivery). Very visual: tiny boats, storms, puffins.
- **Coverage:** **wide open**. Zero YouTube videos found.
- **Sources:**
  - ✅ National Trust for Scotland, "St Kilda mailboat's epic journey" (2020)
  - Sheep-bladder float: St Kilda Club. *Confirm one more source before scripting.*

### 3. An Astrology Company Sued the Two Guys Who Run Every Clock on Earth
**Alt hook:** *"Your phone's clock depends on a volunteer's text file."*

- **Story:**
  - Every phone, laptop and server gets its time-zone rules from one free database (tz). Since 1986 it has been kept by a couple of volunteers, Arthur David Olson and Paul Eggert.
  - In 2011, Astrolabe, an astrology software company, sued them for copyright. Their claim was that historical time-zone facts came from an atlas Astrolabe owned.
  - The EFF defended the volunteers. In February 2012 Astrolabe dropped the case, after being warned it would face sanctions.
- **Ryan & Maya:** Ryan blames his horoscope for being late. Maya shows him that his horoscope's company once tried to own *time*.
- **Why it clicks:** it's CGP Grey territory (invisible infrastructure), plus an absurd villain. The tech/curiosity crowd loves this.
- **Coverage:** **open lane**. Only 1 video found.
- **Sources:**
  - ✅ EFF case page "Astrolabe v. Olson"
  - ✅ Wired (Feb 2012)
  - ✅ Westlaw summary
  - *Verify before scripting:* the server takedown and the ICANN handover detail.

### 4. In 1838, America Took Tomato Pills for Everything. They Contained No Tomato.
**Story beats:**
1. **1834:** Dr. John Cook Bennett announces that tomatoes cure indigestion and more. Tomatoes were seen as suspicious back then.
2. **Late 1830s:** Archibald Miles sells "Dr. Miles' Compound Extract of Tomato", claiming it treats everything from indigestion to syphilis.
3. **The rival:** Dr. Guy R. Phelps, a Yale-trained physician, launches his own tomato pills. Miles accuses Phelps's cheap pills of containing no tomato. Phelps calls Miles "unjust and unmanly".
4. **The punchline:** *neither* product contained tomato. They were basically laxatives.
5. **The crash:** the whole thing collapses in late 1839, once the advertising stops.

- **Ryan & Maya:** Ryan's influencer gummy brand vs. a rival gummy brand. Both are lying.
- **Why it clicks:** a rivalry and a fraud reveal, and it mirrors today's supplement and influencer culture without preaching.
- **Coverage:** **open lane for long-form**. Only Shorts exist.
- **Sources:**
  - ✅ Atlas Obscura, "The Tomato Pill Craze"
  - ✅ Andrew F. Smith, *The Tomato in America*

### 5. Every Christmas Eve at 3pm, Sweden Stops to Watch the Same 1958 Cartoon ⏰ *timely: Christmas is in ~5 weeks*
- **Story:**
  - Since 1959, Swedish TV has shown the 1958 Disney special "From All of Us to All of You" ("Kalle Anka") at 3pm on 24 December.
  - In 2017 it drew 3.8M viewers, nearly 40% of the country, and the 1997 record was over half. In 2024 the figure was 3.09M.
  - Families plan dinner around it. When the broadcaster tried changing clips, people protested.
- **Ryan & Maya:** Ryan tries to start an "unbreakable" office tradition. Maya: "Sweden did it with a duck and zero effort."
- **Why it clicks:** it's a seasonal search spike, and "a whole country does this one weird thing" is a proven format.
- **Coverage:** light. Only a couple of small videos and Shorts.
- **Sources:**
  - ✅ Atlas Obscura
  - ✅ Scandinavia Standard (viewer numbers)
  - ✅ Swedish Wikipedia
  - *Verify the "protest" detail before scripting.*
