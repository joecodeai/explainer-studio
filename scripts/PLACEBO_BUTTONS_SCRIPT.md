## 1. What's working on YouTube right now (vidIQ data, 18 Nov 2026)

The winning pattern: **something the viewer has personally done or seen + a hidden reason behind it**, framed by a hook with a number, an experiment or a paradox. Small channels break out on topic alone:

**Already crowded (avoid):** hotels, all-inclusive resorts, buffets, casinos, cruises, dollar stores, Costco and the Rolex waitlist. Half as Interesting got 1.8M on Rolex in September.

**Our edge over these faceless channels:** they're narrated slideshows. We have two characters who *live* the topic. Ryan does the experiment, gets it wrong and pays for it. The facts become a story, which drives watch time and comments.

## 3. EPISODE — "This Button Has Done Nothing Since 1990"

**Premise:**
- Ryan is a presser. He presses "close door", the crossing button and the office thermostat. He believes pressing is how you get things done.
- Maya shows him that most of these buttons are placebos. He refuses to believe it, so he investigates. Each investigation takes away a button.
- He overcorrects and stops pressing anything, including the one button that works.
- **Ending:** Maya gives him a button that does nothing, on purpose. It works anyway. The last beat is Maya pressing the crossing button herself.

**Running devices:**
- **The PRESS COUNTER**, an on-screen tally of Ryan's presses that climbs all episode.
- **The stopwatch**, used whenever Ryan times a wait.
- **The thermostat war**: Ryan versus Dana, cold versus warm, waged in silence.

*(Tags = voice delivery, as in the GTA script. ⏸ = a written silence between lines, never inside one.)*

### SCENE 1 — COLD OPEN: THE LIFT
*Morning. A small office lift. Ryan and Maya step in. Ryan jabs CLOSE DOOR before the doors have even finished opening. Press counter appears in the corner: **1, 2, 3, 4…***
- **RYAN** `rationalising` *(pressing)*: Every second counts.
- **MAYA** `dry`: That button doesn't do anything.
- **RYAN** `deadpan` *(still pressing)*: It does for me. ⏸ 1.0
*The doors close at exactly the speed they were always going to. Counter: **11**.*
- **RYAN** `rationalising`: See?
- **MAYA** `dry`: They were closing anyway.
- **RYAN** `deadpan`: Because I asked.

**TITLE CARD:** *THIS BUTTON HAS DONE NOTHING SINCE 1990* → *RYAN FIGURES IT OUT*

### SCENE 2 — THE LAW OF THE LIFT
*The lift, moving. A screen insert: an elevator panel with the CLOSE DOOR button highlighted.*
- **MAYA** `dry`: In America, since the disability law of 1990, the doors have to stay open at least three seconds. The close button isn't allowed to make that shorter.
- **RYAN** `curious`: And after three seconds?
- **MAYA** `dry`: Otis, the lift company, says whether the button actually closes the door sooner is up to the building owner.
- **RYAN** `rationalising`: So it might work.
- **MAYA** `dry`: It might.
*Ryan presses it again, more gently, like a man who has made peace with uncertainty. Counter: **12**.*

### SCENE 3 — THE CROSSING
*Outside. A pedestrian crossing. Ryan pounds the button. Counter climbs to **19**. Maya stands next to him, not pressing anything.*
- **RYAN** `earnest`: This one works. It says WAIT. It's talking to me.
- **MAYA** `dry`: In 2004, the New York Times found that more than two thousand five hundred of New York's three thousand two hundred and fifty crossing buttons were, quote, mechanical placebos.
*Screen insert: **2,500 of 3,250**.*
- **RYAN** `deadpan`: We're not in New York.
- **MAYA** `dry`: London has about two and a half thousand crossings at junctions. Transport for London says during the day, the button has no effect.
- **RYAN** `curious`: None?
- **MAYA** `dry`: It works between midnight and seven a.m.
*Ryan looks at the button. Then at his watch. It's 8:52.*
- **RYAN** `deflated`: So I'm only in charge between midnight and seven. ⏸ 0.8
*The light turns green. Ryan crosses. He doesn't look back at the button. The button doesn't look back at him.*

### SCENE 4 — THE THERMOSTAT WAR
*The office. A thermostat on the wall. Ryan walks past and, without breaking stride, turns it down to 19. Five seconds later Dana walks past and turns it back up to 23. Neither acknowledges the other. Screen insert: a ledger titled THERMOSTAT WAR showing **RYAN 214, DANA 211**.*
- **MAYA (V.O. on the phone)** `dry`: How's the office?
- **RYAN** `rationalising` *(on the phone)*: Winning. I've kept this floor at nineteen degrees since March.
- **MAYA** `dry`: Is it nineteen degrees?
*Ryan looks around. Everyone is wearing exactly what they wore yesterday. Dana is wearing a cardigan.*
- **RYAN** `deadpan`: It's a slow war.
- **MAYA** `dry`: In 2003, a heating and air-con trade paper asked contractors if they'd ever installed dummy thermostats. Fifty-one out of seventy said yes.
*Long pause. Ryan looks at the thermostat. Then he gently pulls it off the wall. It comes away easily. There is no wire behind it. There is nothing behind it. Dana walks up at the same moment, sees it, and stops.*
*Ryan and Dana stare at it. Then, in silence, Ryan presses it back onto the wall. They both walk away in opposite directions. Ledger: **RYAN 214, DANA 211. WAR SUSPENDED.***

### SCENE 5 — WHY WE FALL FOR IT
*The flat, evening. Ryan at the table with a notepad headed BUTTONS I TRUSTED. All three entries are crossed out.*
- **RYAN** `defeated`: Why would anyone build a button that does nothing?
- **MAYA** `curious`: Because pressing it feels like doing something.
- **RYAN** `deadpan`: Pressing it IS doing something.
- **MAYA** `dry`: In 1975, a psychologist called Ellen Langer ran a lottery in an office. Some people picked their own ticket. Others were just handed one.
- **RYAN** `curious`: Same odds?
- **MAYA** `dry`: Same odds. When she offered to buy the tickets back, the people who'd picked their own asked for eight dollars sixty-seven. The others asked for one ninety-six.
- **RYAN** `deadpan`: Because they chose it.
- **MAYA** `dry`: She called it the illusion of control.
*Ryan looks down at his phone. A travel app: "Searching 400 airlines for your best price…" with a spinning globe.*
- **RYAN** `research`: This app takes ages. That's how I know it's working hard.
- **MAYA** `dry`: Harvard tested that. People preferred a website that made them wait and showed its work, over one that gave the same results instantly.
- **RYAN** `deflated`: So I like being made to wait.
- **MAYA** `dry`: If someone's watching you do it.
*The spinner keeps spinning. Ryan watches it, betrayed.*


### SCENE 5b — FAKE SOUNDS *(added during the build)*
*The flat. Ryan photographs his dinner. Click.*
- **RYAN** `rationalising`: Dinner.
- **MAYA** `dry`: Your phone doesn't have a shutter. That click is a recording.
- **RYAN** `curious`: Then why does it click?
- **MAYA** `dry`: So you know it worked.
- **RYAN** `deadpan`: It's a placebo noise.
- **MAYA** `dry`: In Japan you can't turn it off. It isn't even a law. The phone industry just agreed on it.
- **RYAN** `deflated`: Who else is lying to me?

*Card: **BMW M5: engine sound played through the car's speakers** ("Active Sound Design", CNET).*
- **MAYA** `dry`: Some BMWs play engine noise through the speakers, so the car sounds the way you expect it to.
- **RYAN** `curious`: And electric cars?
- **MAYA** `dry`: Too quiet. Since July 2021, every new electric car in the EU has to make a noise at low speed, so people on the pavement hear it coming.
- **RYAN** `deadpan`: So the quiet car has to pretend to be loud, and the loud car is pretending to be louder.
- **MAYA** `distracted`: Mm.
- **RYAN** `deflated`: I don't trust anything that clicks.

### SCENE 6 — RYAN STOPS PRESSING
*Next morning. The office lobby. Ryan stands in front of the lift, arms folded, pressing nothing. Press counter frozen at **19**. On-screen: **8:51**.*
*Time-lapse. People arrive and press the call button, and the lift takes them away. Ryan stands there, principled.*
*On-screen: **8:55**.*
- **DANA** `professional` *(arriving, pressing the call button)*: Morning.
- **RYAN** `rationalising`: I don't press buttons any more. They're placebos.
- **DANA** `professional`: That one calls the lift, Ryan.
*The lift arrives instantly for Dana. She gets in. The doors close. Ryan is alone. ⏸ 1.5*
- **RYAN** `under_breath`: ...That one works.
*He presses it. Counter: **20**. He has never pressed anything so quietly.*


### SCENE 7a — THE EXPENSIVE PAINKILLER *(added during the build)*
- **RYAN** `rationalising`: Headache. I'm getting the expensive ones. The cheap ones don't work on me.
- **MAYA** `dry`: In 2008, researchers gave people a fake painkiller. They told half of them it cost two dollars fifty a pill, and the other half that it cost ten cents.
- **RYAN** `curious`: And?
- **MAYA** `dry`: Eighty-five percent of the expensive group said the pain went down. Sixty-one percent of the cheap group.
- **RYAN** `rationalising`: Expensive works.
- **MAYA** `deadpan`: They were both sugar.
- **RYAN** `deflated`: So I'm paying extra for the box.

*(Scene 7 now opens with **MAYA** `dry`: "This one was free.")*

### SCENE 7 — THE BUTTON THAT WORKS
*The flat, evening. Maya puts a big red button on the table in front of Ryan. It isn't connected to anything. A label on it in Maya's handwriting: DOES NOTHING.*
- **RYAN** `curious`: What does it do?
- **MAYA** `dry`: It says on it.
*Ryan looks at it with suspicion. Then he presses it. Click. A pause. His shoulders drop slightly.*
- **RYAN** `deflated` *(quietly)*: ...That helped.
- **MAYA** `dry`: In 2010, Harvard doctors gave patients sugar pills and told them, honestly, they were sugar pills. Fifty-nine percent of them reported their symptoms got better, compared with thirty-five percent who got nothing.
- **RYAN** `curious`: Even though they knew?
- **MAYA** `dry`: Even though they knew.
*Ryan presses the red button again. Counter: **21**. He looks almost peaceful.*

### SCENE 8 — THE LAST CROSSING
*The next morning. The same crossing. Ryan stands, hands in pockets, enlightened, and does not press the button.*
*Maya, standing beside him, glances at him. When she thinks he isn't looking, she quietly presses the button twice.*
*Ryan sees. A long look.*
- **MAYA** `deadpan`: It's a placebo. ⏸ 0.6
- **MAYA** `deadpan`: It still works.
*The light turns green. They cross. Final counter: **RYAN 21 · MAYA 2**.*

**END CARD**
- **MAYA (V.O.)** `narration`: Ryan Figures It Out. Which button do you still press? Tell us. We won't tell the button.

## 5. Fact check (every claim, with source)

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| US doors must stay open ≥3 s; "door close" can't reduce the initial opening time (ADA, 1990) | US Access Board / ADA guidance, quoted by Nexstar/Fox 8, 26 Oct 2024 |
| Otis: whether the button actually closes the door sooner "is also decided by the customer" | Otis to Nexstar, 26 Oct 2024 |
| NYC: >2,500 of 3,250 walk buttons were "mechanical placebos" | New York Times, 27 Feb 2004 |
| London: 4,650 crossings, ~2,500 at junctions; at junctions "in the daytime, the button has no effect"; it works midnight–07:00 | BBC News Magazine, quoting Transport for London |
| 51 of 70 HVAC contractors said they had installed dummy thermostats | ACHR News, "Placebo Stats", 27 Mar 2003 |
| Langer 1975 lottery: chose ticket → mean resale $8.67 vs $1.96 assigned; "illusion of control" | Langer, J. Personality & Social Psychology 1975 (via PMC review) |
| Labor illusion: people preferred sites with longer waits when shown the work, even with identical results | Buell & Norton, Management Science 2011 (Harvard Business School) |
| Open-label placebo: 59% adequate relief vs 35% no-treatment, patients told they were placebos | Kaptchuk et al., PLoS ONE 2010 (Harvard Medical School) |
| Price placebo: told $2.50/pill vs 10 cents → 85% vs 61% reported less pain (all placebo) | Waber, Shiv, Carmon & Ariely, JAMA 2008 |
| EU: sound-emitting device obligatory in all new electric cars from 1 July 2021 (low speeds) | European Commission, 2019 |
| BMW M5 plays engine noise through the stereo (Active Sound Design) | CNET |
| Japan's phone shutter sound is not regulated by government; it's industry practice | Library of Congress, 2008 |

**Invented for the story (not presented as fact):** the press counter, the thermostat-war tallies, the travel app, the 8:52 timestamp, and the claim that the office thermostat has nothing behind it. That last one is Ryan's office only. The survey says *some* thermostats are dummies, and the script never claims all of them are.
