# Benny Figures It Out — show bible (draft 2026-11-16)

**One-line pitch:** Benny is confidently clueless. Every episode something in the world (news, money, dating, career, social life) hits his life, he gets it hilariously wrong, and his best friend Maya, who is also the narrator, walks him and the viewer through how it really works. Benny learns, and so does the audience.

## Why this format
- **A recurring character people like.** Likes come from attachment, not information. Benny is the channel's face: same look, same voice, same flaws in every video.
- **Every topic becomes personal.** The question isn't "what happened in Japan" but "why did Benny's trip just get cheaper?"
- **Not only news.** News episodes bring search and trending traffic. Life episodes (dating, career, money, social) keep earning views for years.
- **A clearly original, story-driven format** with a recurring character and a written comedic arc, so it doesn't look like mass-produced AI news.

## Cast
### Benny — the lead
- **Who:** late 20s and lives alone in a small flat. He has a vague office job ("something in operations") and a goldfish named Dividend.
- **Personality:**
  - handsome and charming, but confidently wrong;
  - takes everything personally and literally;
  - has big plans and a short attention span;
  - sincere, never mean, a lovable underdog.
- **The comedy:**
  - He jumps to the wrong conclusion.
  - He asks the "dumb" question the viewer was secretly wondering.
  - He applies the lesson slightly wrong at the end.
- **Catchphrase:** "Okay, but how does this affect *me*?"
- **Look:** tousled dark hair, warm brown eyes, a bright boyish smile, an orange hoodie over a white T-shirt, jeans, white sneakers and his phone.
- **Voice:** a warm, pleasant young male voice, energetic and very expressive.

### Maya — co-lead and narrator
- **Who:** Benny's smart, witty best friend, also late 20s and stylish. She tells the story in voice-over, so the narrator voice IS Maya.
- **On screen:** she corrects Benny, and their banter is the heart of the show.
- **Personality:** playful, dry and fond of him, and always right about the facts.
- **Look:** long wavy dark hair and a warm confident smile, in an olive bomber jacket, cream top, dark jeans and ankle boots.
- **Voice:** warm, smooth and confident, with a dry wit.

### Guests
- Each episode adds 0–2 guests: a landlord, a boss, a date, a banker, an expert.

## Episode structure (6–8 min long-form)
1. **Cold open (0–6 s):** Benny does something dumb because of the topic. ("Benny just cancelled his Japan trip because he heard the yen 'collapsed'.") Maya (voice-over), deadpan: "The yen collapsing is the *best* thing that's happened to Benny's holiday."
2. **The catchphrase:** "Okay, but how does this affect me?" Then a 1-line promise of what we'll learn.
3. **Benny's wrong theory:** a short, funny scene that sets up the misconception the audience may share.
4. **Maya explains:** 3–4 short chunks of real facts with maps, charts and numbers. After each chunk Benny reacts or asks the next "dumb" question, which is the audience's real question.
5. **How it really affects Benny:** his wallet, job, dating life or plans, with concrete numbers where the sources have them.
6. **Benny tries to use the lesson** and gets it 80% right. Punchline.
7. **Takeaway + sign-off:** Maya gives the one-sentence lesson for the viewer, and Benny asks the audience a question for the comments ("Would you have cancelled the trip? Tell me I'm not the only one").

## Rules
- **Facts:** real and sourced. Benny's mistakes are the joke, never the facts.
- **Who the jokes are on:**
  - Benny and his choices;
  - bureaucracy, markets and officials.
  - Never real victims, deaths or tragedies. Skip topics like that, or keep Benny out of the tragic part.
- **Benny himself:** dumb but never cruel, sexist or offensive. He learns, and he's likeable.
- **Pacing:** one visual change at every new idea, and a Benny reaction about every 20–30 s.

## Topic mix (per 4 videos)
- 2× **news through Benny's life**, e.g. "Petrol prices jumped. Benny bought a horse."
- 1× **money or career**, e.g. "Benny asks for a raise", "Benny discovers compound interest", "Benny's first credit card".
- 1× **life, dating or social**, e.g. "Benny learns why he's being ghosted", "Benny vs. the group-chat bill split", "Benny tries to rent a flat".

## Shorts
- From each long video, cut 3–5 vertical Shorts.
- Each Short is: Benny's dumb take, then one surprising fact, then Benny's reaction.
- End each Short with "Full episode on the channel".

## Titles / thumbnails
- **Titles:** Benny's situation + curiosity, e.g. "Benny Cancelled Japan Because of the Yen (Big Mistake)" or "Why Benny Keeps Getting Ghosted (It's Science)".
- **Thumbnail:** Benny's huge expressive face + one object + max 3 words.

## Comedy rules (what makes it funny without being cringe)
- **What works:**
  - Benny's misplaced priorities ("I could eat ramen three times a day").
  - Oddly specific confessions ("I read the headline. And one comment. The comment was very upset").
  - Maya's dry 2–5 word replies ("Put the suitcase down." / "Dividend is a fish.").
  - Small running details from his life (Dividend the goldfish, free stuff, ramen), called back later.
  - A small tag after a joke instead of a bigger joke.
  - The opening mistake paid off at the end.
- **Aim:** 8–12 real laughs per episode, at least one in every scene with Benny and Maya.
- **Never:**
  - puns or wordplay;
  - "well, well, well", "plot twist", "buckle up", "let that sink in", "game-changer", "vibes";
  - jokes that explain themselves;
  - meta lines about the show;
  - forced memes or pop-culture references;
  - lots of exclamation marks.
- **Dialogue:** short, plain sentences, the way real people talk.
